for View-Graph Calibration

Viktor Larsson1 Johannes Schönberger2

1Lund University    2Meta Reality Labs

Abstract

View-graph calibration jointly estimates camera intrinsics from multiple pairwise fundamental matrices. The correct intrinsics transform each fundamental matrix into an essential matrix, casting the calibration problem as minimizing the distance to the essential manifold.

We study how to represent this objective for nonlinear least squares. The conventional singular-value formulation collapses two independent essential-matrix constraints into one scalar residual. Its per-edge Gauss–Newton model is therefore rank deficient, which leads to poor convergence.

We instead derive an isometric reduction to 2×2 matrices, where each branch of the nonlinear essential-matrix constraint becomes a linear subspace. This reduction yields a smooth two-dimensional residual whose squared norm is exactly the original manifold distance, whose Gauss–Newton approximation is generically full rank, and whose evaluation requires no per-edge singular value decomposition. Experiments show that the new residual retains the estimation accuracy of the geometric objective while converging in fewer, computationally cheaper iterations. Combined with robust initialization and optimization, it achieves state-of-the-art view-graph calibration accuracy at a runtime comparable to inexpensive algebraic objectives.

The problem

View-graph calibration

Estimate the focal length of every image from the pairwise fundamental matrices of a view graph, before any camera poses or 3D points are recovered.

Input

Fundamental matrices \(F_{ij}\) between image pairs, forming a view graph.

→
Constraint
\[A_{ij} = K_j^\top F_{ij}\, K_i\]

For the correct focal lengths, \(A_{ij}\) is an essential matrix: its two non-zero singular values are equal.

→
Optimization
\[\min_{\{f_i\}} \sum_{(i,j)} \rho\big(\ell(A_{ij})\big)\]

Minimize the distance \(\ell\) to the essential manifold over all edges, with a robust loss \(\rho\). Output: a focal length \(f_i\) per image.

The objective

Distance to the essential manifold

A natural choice for \(\ell\) is the normalized distance from \(A_{ij}\) to the nearest essential matrix. It measures geometric inconsistency directly and does not depend on the arbitrary scale of \(F_{ij}\).

The simplest residual for least-squares solvers is the scalar \(r=\sqrt{\ell}\), which requires an SVD per edge and packs the two essential-matrix constraints into a single number.

We reformulate the same distance as two smooth residuals, which give a better local model for Gauss–Newton optimization and do not require computing a 3×3 SVD in each iteration.

\[ \ell(A)=\min_{E\in\mathcal{E}}\frac{\|A-E\|_F^2}{\|A\|_F^2} =\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2}{2\,(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)} \]

\(\mathcal{E}=\{U\operatorname{diag}(\alpha,\alpha,0)V^\top\}\) is the essential manifold and \(\sigma_1\geq\sigma_2\) are the non-zero singular values of \(A\).

Reformulating the essential-manifold distance

Keep the geometric objective, change how it is factored into residuals.

01

Minimizing the essential-manifold distance

Nonlinear least-squares solvers such as Gauss–Newton or Levenberg–Marquardt minimize a sum of squared residuals, \(\sum_{(i,j)}\|r(A_{ij})\|^2\), by repeatedly linearizing each residual \(r\). To use the distance in such a solver, it must be written as a residual with \(\|r(A)\|^2=\ell(A)\). The natural choice is the scalar residual

\[ r(A)=\sqrt{\ell(A)}=\frac{\sigma_1-\sigma_2}{\sqrt{2\,(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)}}. \]

This has several problems:

  • Non-differentiable at the solution. It behaves like an absolute value where \(\sigma_1=\sigma_2\).
  • One residual for a codimension-two constraint. Its Gauss–Newton model has rank one.
  • Requires an SVD. A 3×3 SVD of \(A\) has to be computed in each iteration.
02

Isometric reduction to 2×2

Let \(a\) and \(b\) be unit left and right null vectors of the rank-2 matrix \(A\), so that \(A^\top a=0\) and \(Ab=0\). Let \(P_a,P_b\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 3}\) have orthonormal rows spanning the complements \(a^\perp\) and \(b^\perp\). Projecting onto them gives a 2×2 matrix \(C = P_a A P_b^\top\). Since \(A = P_a^\top C P_b\), no component of \(A\) is lost.

\(P_a\)2×3
·
\(A\)3×3, rank 2
·
\(P_b^\top\)3×2
=
\(C\)2×2

Recall that the essential manifold \(\mathcal{E}=\{U\operatorname{diag}(\alpha,\alpha,0)V^\top:\ \alpha>0,\ U,V\in\mathrm{O}(3)\}\) consists of the rank-2 matrices with two equal singular values.

The key observation is now that \(\boldsymbol{A}\) being an essential matrix is equivalent to \(\boldsymbol{C}\) being scaled orthogonal, i.e.:

\(A\in\mathcal{E}\) \(\Longleftrightarrow\) \(C\in\{\alpha R:\ \alpha\geq 0,\ R\in\mathrm{O}(2)\}\)

Scaled \(\mathrm{O}(2)\) is the union of two orthogonal linear subspaces, the scaled rotations and the scaled reflections:

\[ \mathcal{S}_+=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\-\beta&\alpha\end{bmatrix}\right\},\quad \mathcal{S}_-=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\beta&-\alpha\end{bmatrix}\right\}. \]
03

A smooth two-component residual

The distance to the nearer subspace is a smooth 2-vector:

\[ r_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\|A_{ij}\|_F} \begin{bmatrix} c_{11}-\eta\, c_{22}\\ c_{12}+\eta\, c_{21}\end{bmatrix}. \]

Here \(\eta=\operatorname{sign}\det C\) selects the nearer of the two subspaces. Its squared norm is exactly the original distance, \(\|r_{ij}\|^2=\ell(A_{ij})\), so the objective is unchanged.

04

Parameterizing the projections

The reduction needs the null spaces of \(A_{ij}\), but these never have to be recomputed during optimization. The null vectors \(a_{ij},b_{ij}\) of \(F_{ij}\) are computed once per edge, and the null vectors of \(A_{ij}\) follow by transport through the current calibration:

\[ \hat a=\frac{K_j^{-1}a_{ij}}{\|K_j^{-1}a_{ij}\|},\qquad \hat b=\frac{K_i^{-1}b_{ij}}{\|K_i^{-1}b_{ij}\|}. \]

Writing \(a_{ij}=(a_1,a_2,a_3)^\top\) and \((u,v)\) for the principal point, \(K_j^{-1}a_{ij}\propto(a_1-ua_3,\ a_2-va_3,\ f_j a_3)^\top\). As \(f_j\) varies, \(\hat a\) stays in the fixed plane spanned by \(e_3\) and \(w=(a_1-ua_3,\ a_2-va_3,\ 0)^\top\). The axis \(n_a\propto w\times e_3\) is therefore orthogonal to \(\hat a\) for every focal length, which gives the projection in closed form:

\[ P_a=\begin{bmatrix} n_a^\top\\ (\hat a\times n_a)^\top\end{bmatrix}, \]

and likewise for \(P_b\). The projections vary smoothly with the focal lengths, and the branch \(\eta\) is fixed at initialization because \(|\det C|=\sigma_1\sigma_2>0\) cannot change sign along an optimization path. Every step is an elementary matrix expression: no SVD inside the solver loop.
